Inspiration for Zenos

Make the unimaginable imaginable

Our vision and the way we work all originate from one of the oldest puzzles in philosophy: Zeno's paradox. The Greek philosopher who formulated a paradox to challenge our understanding of motion and infinity.

01 · The paradox

Achilles never wins

In the paradox, Achilles chases a tortoise. Each time he arrives at its previous position, it has moved on, so how can he ever catch up?

Read more

Zeno of Elea (c. 490–430 BC) gives the tortoise a head start. To pass it, Achilles must first reach the point where it started. By then, the tortoise has crawled a little further, so Achilles must reach that point too, and so on. There is always another gap to close. Zeno concluded that infinitely many steps can never be completed, so Achilles can never win. We know he does, yet the argument puzzled thinkers for over two thousand years.

02 · The solution

Wrong reasoning

The race was never impossible; the reasoning was.

Read more

The flaw is the assumption that infinitely many steps must take infinitely long. Each gap Achilles closes is smaller than the last, and so is the time it takes. The mathematics of convergent series, formalised in the 17th to 19th centuries, shows that such a sum can be finite: ½ + ¼ + ⅛ + … = 1. Achilles passes the tortoise at a precise, calculable moment.

03 · Our view

Physiology is no different

Solving physiological questions with models now has its limits. However, the right approach can extend our reach beyond what is currently possible.

Read more

Today's models of the body are mostly statistical: they learn patterns from measured data and break down outside it. Many important situations, such as heat stress combined with severe blood loss, are too dangerous to measure. As with Zeno, the limit lies in the method, not the question. A mechanistic model, built on the physical laws that govern the body, holds in every condition and can reason beyond the data it was built on.

The search for the ultimate model

Our long-term goal is a mathematical model that integrates all of human physiology: every organ system, connected through the same physical laws. We won't get there in one leap. Like Achilles, we close the distance step by step: each version of the model covers more of the body and is tested against real measurements before we take the next step.

Every step brings us closer. Enough steps, and we arrive.
